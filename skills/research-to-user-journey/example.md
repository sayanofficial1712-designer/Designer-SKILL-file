# User Journey Example

> This example demonstrates the expected output of the Research to User Journey workflow using a food delivery application.

---

# Journey Overview

**Journey Name:** Ordering Dinner through a Food Delivery App

**Primary Actor:** Busy Working Professional

**Scenario:** The user wants to order dinner after a long day at work using a food delivery application.

**Primary Goal:** Order food quickly with minimal effort and receive it on time.

**Success Criteria:**

- User finds a suitable restaurant.
- User completes payment successfully.
- Food is delivered within the promised ETA.
- Overall experience is smooth and satisfying.

**Research Inputs:**

- 10 User Interviews
- 5 Usability Testing Sessions
- Customer Support Tickets
- Product Analytics
- Observation Notes

---

# User Journey Map

| | Browse Restaurants | Compare Options | Place Order | Track Delivery | Receive Order |
|-----------------|----------------|----------------|----------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Actions** | Opens app and browses restaurants | Compares ratings, prices and delivery time | Adds items to cart and pays | Tracks driver's location | Receives food |
| **Touchpoints** | Home Feed | Restaurant Page | Cart & Payment | Live Tracking Screen | Delivery Partner |
| **Thoughts** | "What should I eat today?" | "Is this worth the price?" | "Hope my payment goes through." | "Why is it taking so long?" | "Finally, my food is here." |
| **Emotions** | Curious | Indecisive | Hopeful | Frustrated | Happy |
| **Pain Points** | Too many restaurant options | Hidden delivery fees | Payment failures | ETA changes frequently | Missing items in the order |
| **Opportunities** | Better recommendations | Price comparison | Simplify checkout | Improve ETA prediction | Delivery verification |

---

# Key Insights

- Users spend significantly more time choosing food than placing the order.
- Hidden delivery charges reduce trust.
- Delivery tracking reduces anxiety but only when the ETA is accurate.
- Payment failures have a disproportionate impact on user satisfaction.
- Repeat users prefer shortcuts over discovery.

---

# Evidence & Supporting Anecdotes

Summarise the evidence supporting the generated journey. Include user quotes, observations and behavioural anecdotes wherever possible.

## Strong Evidence

Supported by multiple participants and research methods.

- 8 out of 10 participants compared at least three restaurants before deciding.
- 9 out of 10 checked delivery time before looking at reviews.
- Analytics showed the restaurant comparison screen had the highest average time spent.

### Supporting Anecdotes

> "I usually open three restaurants before deciding."

> "Delivery time matters more than ratings."

---

## Moderate Evidence

Supported by repeated observations but limited participants.

- Returning users preferred reordering previous meals.
- Some users ignored promotional banners completely.

### Supporting Anecdotes

> "I always order the same biryani every Friday."

---

## Weak / Missing Evidence

Areas requiring further validation.

### Missing Evidence

- No accessibility research.
- No research with users above 60 years old.
- Limited observations for rural users.
- No cross-platform behavioural comparison.

---

# Design Recommendations

## Short-term Recommendations

High-impact improvements that can be implemented quickly.

- Show final price earlier in the flow.
- Reduce the number of checkout steps.
- Improve ETA accuracy.
- Allow quick reordering from the homepage.

---

## Long-term Recommendations

Strategic improvements requiring broader investment.

- AI-powered personalised restaurant recommendations.
- Predictive delivery estimation.
- Adaptive homepage based on ordering behaviour.
- Smart meal planning based on previous purchases.

---

# Open Questions

Questions requiring further research.

- Why do users abandon after comparing multiple restaurants?
- Does free delivery influence decisions more than ratings?
- Would users trust AI-generated meal recommendations?
- How does behaviour differ between first-time and repeat customers?

Assumptions made while generating this journey.
