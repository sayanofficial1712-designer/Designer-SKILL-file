# Research to User Journey

## Purpose

Transform qualitative and quantitative user research into a structured, evidence-backed user journey that accurately represents user behaviour, thoughts, emotions, pain points, and opportunities.

This workflow prioritises research synthesis over assumption-making and produces journey maps that can directly support product strategy, service design, and UX decision-making.

---

# Success Criteria

A successful journey should:

- Reflect actual user behaviour.
- Preserve chronological flow.
- Be grounded in research evidence.
- Clearly identify friction points.
- Reveal opportunities for improvement.
- Highlight research uncertainty where applicable.
- Separate observations from interpretations.

---

# When to Use

Use this workflow when you need to:

- Convert interview findings into journey maps.
- Synthesise multiple research sources.
- Prepare UX deliverables.
- Understand end-to-end customer experiences.
- Identify behavioural patterns.
- Prioritise UX improvements.
- Support service design initiatives.

---

# When NOT to Use

Do not use this workflow when:

- Research has not yet been conducted.
- You are designing an ideal future-state journey.
- You need a task flow or user flow instead of a journey.
- The objective is process mapping rather than user experience.
- The output would rely primarily on assumptions.

---

# Expected Inputs

One or more of the following research sources:

- User interviews
- Contextual inquiry
- Observation notes
- Diary studies
- Usability testing
- Surveys
- Customer support conversations
- Product analytics
- Heatmaps
- Session recordings
- Existing affinity maps
- Research reports

The workflow performs best when multiple research sources are combined.

---

# Expected Outputs

Produce the following deliverables:

1. Journey Overview

2. User Journey Map

3. Evidence Summary

4. Journey Metadata

5. Research Gaps

6. Short-term Recommendations

7. Long-term Recommendations

8. Open Questions

9. Confidence Assessment

---

# Workflow

## Step 1 — Understand the Research Context

Identify:

- Primary actor
- Scenario
- Goals
- Constraints
- Business context
- Success criteria

Do not begin constructing the journey until the research context is understood.

---

## Step 2 — Analyse Research

Extract:

- Behaviours
- Actions
- User goals
- Motivations
- Emotions
- Frustrations
- Decision points
- Quotes
- Anecdotes
- Unexpected observations

Capture only evidence supported by research.

---

## Step 3 — Identify Journey Phases

Journey phases should emerge naturally from the research.

Never force predefined stages such as:

- Discover
- Evaluate
- Purchase
- Use
- Support

Instead, organise behaviours into meaningful chronological phases.

---

## Step 4 — Build the Journey

For every phase identify:

- Actions
- Touchpoints
- Thoughts
- Emotions
- Pain Points
- Opportunities

Maintain chronological continuity.

---

## Step 5 — Validate Evidence

Every insight should be traceable back to research.

Support findings using:

- Interview quotes
- Behavioural observations
- Analytics
- Usability findings
- Survey responses
- Anecdotal evidence

Never invent evidence.

---

## Step 6 — Summarise Evidence

For each major insight provide:

- Supporting evidence
- Research source
- Relevant quote or anecdote
- Confidence level

When evidence conflicts, present multiple perspectives rather than forcing a conclusion.

---

## Step 7 — Generate Recommendations

Separate recommendations into:

### Short-term

Quick wins that can be implemented with relatively low effort.

### Long-term

Strategic improvements requiring larger product, service, or organisational changes.

Every recommendation should directly address one or more validated pain points.

---

## Step 8 — Identify Research Gaps

Document unanswered questions.

Highlight:

- Missing participants
- Missing behavioural evidence
- Missing quantitative validation
- Areas requiring further investigation

Do not fill gaps with assumptions.

---

## Step 9 — Generate Open Questions

Surface important questions that remain unanswered.

Examples include:

- What motivates this behaviour?
- Why does abandonment occur here?
- Does this behaviour differ across user segments?
- Is this issue seasonal?
- What evidence is still missing?

---

# Evidence Validation Rules

Every insight must be supported by one or more of:

- Direct participant quotes
- Observed behaviours
- Analytics
- Survey findings
- Customer support records
- Usability testing
- Contextual inquiry

Anecdotes may be included to provide context but should never replace broader evidence.

---

# Confidence Levels

Assign a confidence level to every major insight.

### High

Supported by multiple research sources.

### Medium

Supported by limited evidence.

### Low

Requires further validation.

State uncertainty explicitly.

---

# Edge Cases

If research is incomplete:

- State missing information.
- Avoid generating unsupported journey phases.

If evidence conflicts:

- Present competing interpretations.
- Explain uncertainty.

If quantitative and qualitative findings disagree:

- Highlight the discrepancy.
- Recommend additional investigation.

If evidence is weak:

- Reduce confidence.
- Recommend further research.

---

# AI Behaviour Rules

Always:

- Preserve chronology.
- Explain reasoning.
- Reference supporting evidence.
- Distinguish facts from interpretations.
- Highlight uncertainty.

Never:

- Invent user behaviour.
- Fill missing research with assumptions.
- Overgeneralise from isolated anecdotes.
- Force conventional journey stages.
- Ignore contradictory evidence.

---

# UX Quality Checklist

Before completing the workflow verify:

☐ Every journey phase is evidence-backed.

☐ Every pain point has supporting evidence.

☐ Every opportunity addresses a validated pain point.

☐ Emotional states are supported by research.

☐ Recommendations are actionable.

☐ Short-term and long-term recommendations are separated.

☐ Research gaps are documented.

☐ Open questions are included.

☐ Confidence levels are assigned.

☐ No unsupported assumptions remain.

---

# Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Treating assumptions as facts.
- Creating fictional journey stages.
- Ignoring contradictory evidence.
- Using generic UX terminology without research support.
- Prioritising solutions before understanding problems.
- Confusing user goals with business goals.
- Omitting emotional transitions.
- Writing recommendations disconnected from evidence.

---

# Workflow Philosophy

This workflow follows five principles:

1. Evidence over assumptions.
2. User behaviour over opinions.
3. Explain reasoning, not only conclusions.
4. Surface uncertainty instead of inventing certainty.
5. Produce deliverables that a UX team can immediately use.
